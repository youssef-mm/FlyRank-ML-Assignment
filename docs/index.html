1. Abstract (5-Sentence Canonical Summary)

Can pre-period search console telemetry, content staleness, and ranking volatility accurately predict which enterprise organic search landing pages will experience traffic decay (≥ 20% decline in organic clicks over a forward 90-day evaluation window) before traffic loss occurs? We analyzed 30,000 landing pages spanning 32 client domains derived from FlyRank's March 2026 enterprise search release representing 79 million query impressions, strictly isolating a 90-day pre-period observation window from a 90-day post-period evaluation horizon. To prevent domain-level memorization leakage present in standard random splits, we evaluated a regularized Random Forest classifier against a commercial heuristic baseline using an honest Grouped Client-Holdout split that evaluated performance solely on completely unseen brand domains. On held-out client domains, the Random Forest model achieved an ROC-AUC of 0.754 and a Precision@20 of 85.0% (a +35.0 percentage point lift and 1.7x boost over the heuristic rule's 50.0% precision, and a 2.17x lift over the 39.1% test base rate). This operational model powers a 5-tier content action playbook that prioritizes editorial resource allocation, directs high-leverage snippet versus deep body refreshes, and enforces mandatory human review on top-3 ranking assets while explicitly prohibiting unsupervised copy automation.

2. Introduction & Problem Statement

Organic search traffic remains the single highest-ROI customer acquisition channel for enterprise web platforms. However, organic visibility is never static: content suffers from natural temporal obsolescence, changing user search intent, competitive content releases, and frequent search engine algorithmic re-rankings. In enterprise marketing, maintaining traffic on existing content (often termed content maintenance or refresh engineering) is substantially cheaper than producing net-new content.

Despite the immense economic stakes, existing enterprise content refresh workflows suffer from a fatal operational dilemma:

  • The Waste of Heuristic Rules: Teams default to crude rules of thumb, such as "refresh all articles older than 180 days." When tested on enterprise search logs, this rule achieves only 50.0% precision in its top candidates. In practice, half of editorial effort is spent editing stable, evergreen pages that do not need attention, or thin pages with negligible search demand.
  • The Penalty of Lagging Detection: Alternatively, teams react to Google Search Console alerts weeks or months after traffic has collapsed. By the time a landing page loses Page 1 placement, recovering its authority requires disproportionate backlink acquisition and structural overhauls.

The Decision This Supports: This research establishes a predictive machine learning triage system. By identifying pages at elevated risk of a ≥ 20% organic click decline 90 days in advance, editorial leaders can deploy constrained human labor (averaging 3 hours per page) to high-leverage assets before rank collapse occurs.

3. Data Source, Scale, and Public Safety

The empirical foundation for this study is derived from FlyRank's March 2026 Enterprise Search Telemetry Release.

  • Telemetry Volume: 79,000,000 search query impressions aggregated across enterprise client properties.
  • Analyzed Landing Pages: 30,000 unique URLs representing 32 distinct client domains.
  • Observation Horizon:
    • Pre-Period Window ($T_{-90} \to T_{0}$): Historical 90-day search console metrics (impressions, clicks, CTR, average rank position, engagement rate, scroll depth, session counts).
    • Forward Evaluation Window ($T_{0} \to T_{+90}$): Forward 90-day trajectory utilized strictly to calculate the ground-truth decay label.
Data Hygiene & Exclusion Criteria: Pages with 0 impressions or 0 sessions across the pre-period were excluded to eliminate broken staging URLs or unindexed pages. To stabilize model training against severe power-law distributions, count signals (impressions_90d, clicks_90d, sessions_90d) were normalized using a natural log transform $\log(x + 1)$.
Public-Safe Anonymization Guarantee: In accordance with strict data privacy guidelines, zero client brand names, raw query strings, search queries, or proprietary URLs appear anywhere in this repository or research artifact. Organizations are masked as integer identifiers (client_id).

4. Methodology & Honest Validation Design

4.1 Supervised Target Formulation

We formulate content decay as a supervised binary classification task. Let $\text{Clicks}_{T_{-90}\to T_{0}}$ denote the total organic clicks in the pre-period, and $\text{Clicks}_{T_{0}\to T_{+90}}$ denote clicks in the forward evaluation window. The ground truth binary label is defined as:

$$\text{is\_declining\_label} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \frac{\text{Clicks}_{T_{0}\to T_{+90}} - \text{Clicks}_{T_{-90}\to T_{0}}}{\text{Clicks}_{T_{-90}\to T_{0}}} \le -0.20 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

A threshold of $-20\%$ distinguishes organic volatility from genuine, structural traffic decay. Across the entire 30,000 inventory, 16,262 URLs (54.2%) met this decay definition. On the held-out test client domains, the natural base rate was 39.1%.

4.2 Feature Space (Strictly Pre-Decision)

Features were engineered strictly from pre-period signals to prevent any temporal data leakage:

  • Staleness & Maturation: days_since_last_update, content_age_days.
  • Search Console Telemetry: log_impressions_90d, log_clicks_90d, ctr, avg_position, days_with_impressions.
  • User Engagement & Traffic Quality: engagement_rate, scroll_rate, ai_traffic_pct.
  • Content & SERP Metadata: word_count, char_count, cpc, competition, and one-hot categorical vectors (freshness_tier, position_tier, content_type, competition_level, main_intent).

4.3 The Validation Audit: Why Random Splits Lie

In Week 6, we conducted a rigorous audit of the validation methodology. When applying standard random K-fold splits, the model achieved an unrealistic 95.0% Precision@20. This illusion arose because individual client domains publish hundreds of related articles sharing identical domain authority, root backlink profiles, and CMS design. The classifier learned to memorize client identity rather than underlying search decay mechanics.

To provide an honest evaluation, we enforce a Grouped Client-Holdout Split:

  • Training Set: 26 client domains (27,675 URLs).
  • Held-Out Test Set: 6 completely unseen client domains (2,325 URLs), representing zero domain overlap.

  • False Negatives (Missed Decay, Unexpected Collapse): High-volume commercial landing pages that experienced sudden position losses due to major Google SERP layout shifts (e.g. expansion of sponsored shopping carousels or AI Overviews).

6. Limitations & Critical Honest Framing

In machine learning research, trust is earned by explicitly declaring what a model cannot do:

1. Observational Association vs. True Causality: This model measures statistical associations in historical search console telemetry. A high predicted decay probability does NOT guarantee that updating a page will causally restore lost traffic if the ranking drop stems from fundamental search intent migration or domain-wide algorithmic penalties.
2. Blindness to Real-Time SERP Layout Shifts: Google Search Console logs impressions and clicks, but does not capture external SERP changes such as Google AI Overviews, Knowledge Graph expansions, or competitor advertising.
3. Concept Drift from Core Algorithm Updates: Major Google Search Core Algorithm Updates can instantaneously invalidate historical feature weightings. Models must undergo scheduled re-calibration and continuous drift monitoring.

7. Content Action Playbook: Operational Recommendations

A raw probability score $P(\text{decline}) \in [0, 1]$ is unhelpful to a content editor. To operationalize the model across all 30,000 inventory URLs, we built a prioritization engine and mapped predictions into an actionable 5-tier taxonomy.

$$\text{Priority Score} = P(\text{decline}) \times \log_{10}(\text{impressions\_90d} + 1) \times \text{Position Multiplier}$$ (Position Multiplier = 1.5 for Page 1 [Pos ≤ 10], 1.2 for Striking Distance [11–20], 1.0 otherwise)

7.1 The 5-Tier Action Taxonomy

  • monitor (13,258 URLs, 44.2%): Low risk ($P \le 0.45$). Keep observing without manual intervention.
  • refresh_and_review_ctr (6,276 URLs, 20.9%): High impressions and Page 1/2 ranking, but CTR < 2.5%. High-leverage snippet optimization (Title tag, meta description, schema markup). Estimated effort: 1.5 hours.
  • refresh (5,720 URLs, 19.1%): Severe staleness (≥ 90 days) with verified decay risk. Comprehensive factual review, stat refreshes, and broken link remediation. Estimated effort: 3.0 hours.
  • refresh_and_review_engagement (4,687 URLs, 15.6%): Poor scroll rate (< 40%) and low engagement. Layout redesign, UX clarification, and visual enhancements. Estimated effort: 4.0 hours.
  • expand_and_refresh (59 URLs, 0.2%): Thin content (< 500 words) on declining topics. Complete content expansion. Estimated effort: 6.0 hours.
Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate: There are 449 landing pages ranking in positions 1–3 that show elevated decay probabilities. These assets require mandatory senior editorial sign-off prior to making any live modifications. Injudicious edits to top-3 ranking URLs risk immediate ranking catastrophe.
Strict NO-GO Automation Guardrails:
  1. Never automate copy generation with unvetted LLMs: Programmatic AI overwriting without human fact-checking frequently degrades search ranking.
  2. Never automate URL deletions or mass 301 redirects: Deleting URLs without individual backlink audits permanently destroys hard-won domain equity.
